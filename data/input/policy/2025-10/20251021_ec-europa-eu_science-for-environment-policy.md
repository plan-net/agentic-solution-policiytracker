[Join our free service](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/env/user-subscriptions/1701/create)

**Science for Environment Policy** (SfEP) is a free news and information service published by the Directorate-General for Environment (DG ENV) of the European Commission. It is designed to help busy policymakers keep up-to-date with the latest environmental research findings needed to design, implement and regulate effective policies.

Our News alert articles, Research Spotlights, and Future Briefs are freely accessible through this site. To access SfEP content published between 2005 and June 2022, please consult [the dedicated archive](https://wayback.archive-it.org/12090/20220804144811/https:/ec.europa.eu/environment/integration/research/newsalert/index_en.htm).

The bi-weekly **News Alerts** are a cornerstone of the SfEP service. Presented in concise and easy-to-understand newsletter articles, SfEP brings the latest updates on key environmental research right into your inbox.

## Research Spotlights

**Research Spotlights** is a carefully curated collection of other scientific papers that could be of relevance to our readers. [See all Research Spotlights](https://ec.europa.eu/research-and-innovation/science-environment-policy/research-spotlights_en) curated by Science for Environment Policy.

SfEP also releases regular **Future Briefs** on the latest environmental topics, such as new technologies and innovations and their impact on the environment. Future Briefs are a series of horizon-scanning policy briefs, which explore the evidence around emerging environmental issues.

[Rooting by wild boar can help maintain biodiversity in priority calcareous grasslands, but management must be considered carefully](https://ec.europa.eu/news/rooting-wild-boar-can-help-maintain-biodiversity-priority-calcareous-grasslands-management-must-be-2025-10-27_en)

Researchers find that areas of an unmanaged calcareous grassland in Germany that have been rooted by wild boar have higher plant species richness and diversity, but over-rooting can occur.

[Conservation paradoxes: 36 species which are invasive in some areas but still need protecting elsewhere.](https://ec.europa.eu/news/conservation-paradoxes-36-species-which-are-invasive-some-areas-still-need-protecting-elsewhere-2025-10-21_en)

Some species are invasive and economically costly in some areas, but conservation priorities in others. A study identifies 36 such species, finding them to be conservation priorities due to their threatened status or their distinctiveness.

- 21 October 2025

[Natural disturbances can boost bird diversity in spruce plantations – but management actions shape who thrives](https://ec.europa.eu/news/natural-disturbances-can-boost-bird-diversity-spruce-plantations-management-actions-shape-who-2025-10-21_en)

Natural disturbances created by fire, wind or insect outbreaks in plantations can increase habitat opportunities for declining bird species in central Europe.

- News article
- 23 September 2025

[Acceptance of sustainable solar energy systems is influenced by household age](https://ec.europa.eu/news/acceptance-sustainable-solar-energy-systems-influenced-household-age-2025-09-23_en)

Household age is often overlooked when considering ways to encourage uptake of solar energy systems and increase their circularity. A study addresses this gap, finding that interest in and adoption of solar energy rises to age 65 before declining.
