# ECJ Case: VAT treatment of app stores clarified in XYRALITY GmbH judgment

The Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) has issued a key ruling in _Finanzamt Hamburg-Altona v XYRALITY GmbH_, clarifying how value-added tax (VAT) applies to digital platforms and online marketplaces distributing digital services.

The decision addresses who is regarded as the supplier of services in transactions involving **app developers** and **digital platforms** such as app stores, and how VAT should be applied in such cases.

### Key findings of the judgment

- **Deemed supplier rule (Article 28, EU VAT Directive):** The Court confirmed that where a digital platform (app store) distributes applications or in-app content on behalf of developers, the platform is deemed to have received and supplied those services to final customers.
- **Developer’s supply to the platform:** The developer is treated as supplying the service to the app store, not directly to end users.
- **Place of supply (Article 44):** The developer’s deemed supply occurs where the platform is established. In this case, since the app store was based in Ireland, the place of supply was Ireland, not Germany.
- **VAT liability:** The platform (app store) is responsible for charging and remitting VAT on sales to final customers. The developer applies the reverse charge for its deemed business-to-business supply to the platform.

The case involved XYRALITY GmbH, a German developer of mobile games distributed through an Irish app store. While the apps were free to download, users paid for in-app purchases and upgrades. The dispute arose over whether the platform or the developer should be treated as the supplier for VAT purposes.

The CJEU ruled that the **app store acts as the supplier** since:

- Users interact and transact directly with the store, not the developer.
- The app is available immediately after download via the store.
- The store confirms the contract and handles payment.

### Practical implications

**For developers and suppliers:**

- They are not directly liable for VAT on end-user sales when platforms are deemed suppliers.
- Accurate contractual and documentary arrangements are essential to determine the correct VAT treatment.

**For digital platforms:**

- They carry VAT responsibility for sales to end consumers under Article 28.
- Correct charging, reporting, and compliance with VAT obligations is required.

The ruling provides important clarity for **digital marketplaces, app stores, SaaS platforms, and online content distributors**, ensuring consistent VAT treatment across the EU for platform-based transactions.
