# EU investigates Meta and TikTok for DSA breaches

_Ongoing investigations reveal that Meta and TikTok may have violated the EU’s Digital Services Act by restricting data access and neglecting user complaint systems._

The European Commission [has accused](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_25_2503) Meta and TikTok of breaching the Digital Services Act (DSA), highlighting failures in handling illegal content and providing researchers access to public data.

Meta’s Facebook and Instagram were found to make it too difficult for users to report illegal content or receive responses to complaints, the Commission said in its preliminary findings.

Investigations began after complaints to Ireland’s content regulator, where Meta’s [EU](https://dig.watch/actor/european-union) base is located. The Commission’s inquiry, which has been ongoing since last year, aims to ensure that large platforms protect users and meet EU safety obligations.

[Meta and TikTok](https://dig.watch/updates/cjeu-annuls-dsa-fees-for-meta-and-tiktok) can submit counterarguments before penalties of up to six percent of global annual turnover are imposed.

Both companies face separate concerns about denying researchers adequate access to platform data and preventing oversight of systemic online risks. TikTok is under further examination for minor protection and advertising transparency issues.

The [Commission](https://dig.watch/actor/european-commission) has launched 14 such [DSA](https://dig.watch/updates/eu-warns-meta-and-tiktok-over-transparency-failures)-related proceedings, none concluded.
