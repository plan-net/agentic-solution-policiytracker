# New EU Law Forces Brands to Give Every Garment a Digital ID

By [Sustainability Directory](https://news.sustainability-directory.com/author/sustainability_directory/)_October 5th, 2025_[No Comments](https://news.sustainability-directory.com/fashion/new-eu-law-forces-brands-to-give-every-garment-a-digital-id/#respond)3 min

The European Union’s Ecodesign for [Sustainable Products](https://news.sustainability-directory.com/news/sustainable-products/) Regulation (ESPR) is mandating the Digital Product Passport (DPP) for [textiles](https://fashion.sustainability-directory.com/), a measure that will transform the supply chain from an opaque network into a fully traceable, data-backed system. This means every garment placed on the EU market must carry a unique digital ID, accessible via a QR code, which details its material composition, production processes, and end-of-life instructions like repair and recycling guidelines. The regulation directly addresses the massive waste problem by making product circularity a legal requirement, which is critical given that the EU currently generates 12.6 million tonnes of textile waste per year, with only 22% collected separately for re-use or recycling.

For years, the informed consumer has been asking → “Is supply chain transparency just a marketing claim?” Brands have struggled to provide verifiable proof of a garment’s origin beyond their immediate, Tier 1 suppliers, leaving a massive data gap that enabled greenwashing and obscured environmental and social impacts. The lack of a standardized, mandatory system meant that even when a brand claimed a product was “recycled” or “ethically sourced,” there was no simple, universal way for a consumer or a regulator to verify the claim from [raw material](https://news.sustainability-directory.com/news/raw-material/) to final product.

This change is driven by the EU’s regulatory push to shift the entire textile industry from a linear “take-make-dispose” model to a circular one. The DPP acts as a mandatory digital birth certificate and maintenance log for a garment. Think of it like the VIN (Vehicle Identification Number) for a car → it tracks the entire history, making it impossible to hide the origin of materials or the environmental impact of manufacturing. Brands must now invest heavily in digital infrastructure to map their [supply chains](https://news.sustainability-directory.com/news/supply-chains/) down to the raw material level.

Manufacturers are expected to react by redesigning products for durability and repairability, as the DPP will make a product’s end-of-life fate → landfill or recycling → transparent and traceable. This new dynamic makes [transparency](https://news.sustainability-directory.com/news/transparency/) a non-negotiable legal requirement. It shifts the status of transparency from a voluntary marketing choice to a mandatory compliance metric.

- **Mandate Driver** → EU Ecodesign for Sustainable Products [Regulation](https://news.sustainability-directory.com/news/regulation/) (ESPR).
- **Target Product Group** → All textile and [apparel](https://fashion.sustainability-directory.com/) products placed on the EU market.
- **Key Data Point** → Only 22% of post-consumer [textile waste](https://news.sustainability-directory.com/news/textile-waste/) is currently collected separately for re-use or recycling in the EU.
- **Enforcement Timeline** → Specific requirements expected by January 2026, with enforcement beginning around July 2027.

The DPP is the foundation for a truly circular [fashion](https://fashion.sustainability-directory.com/) system. It implies that [future](https://prism.sustainability-directory.com/) product design will be dictated by data, prioritizing materials that are easily identifiable and recyclable. To see if this trend is continuing, watch for major brands to announce partnerships with technology providers specializing in [supply chain mapping](https://news.sustainability-directory.com/news/supply-chain-mapping/) and digital product identifiers. The next signal will be the release of the first official DPP data standards, which will define exactly what information → such as water usage per garment or repair instructions → brands must legally disclose.

**The era of opaque supply chains is ending; you will soon be able to scan a QR code to verify a garment’s entire environmental and social history.**
