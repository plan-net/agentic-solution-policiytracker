# EU ends tariff exemption on cheap Temu, Shein parcels – What it means for shoppers

By **[Natascha Rivera](https://euroweeklynews.com/author/natascha-r/)** •
Published: 14 Nov 2025 • 10:41
• 2 minutes read

EU abolishes €150 exemption, reshaping online shopping and boosting local sales.
Photo Credit: appshunter.io via Unsplash

THE EU has done away with the tariff exemption on small orders under €150 from popular online shops from China, and in particular Temu and Shein. The new agreement, which was reached on the morning of Thursday, November 13, will re-establish tariffs on small packages and affect not only the aforementioned Chinese e-commerce platforms, but shoppers all over Spain as well.

## Small Chinese parcels overwhelming the European market

[Shein and Temu](https://euroweeklynews.com/2025/07/21/eu-commission-shocked-by-shein-temu-goods-risks/), to name the most prominent Chinese shopping platforms, are massively popular in Europe for their mind-bogglingly cheap goods. In the EU, an incredible 4.6 billion parcels were imported in 2024, with 91 per cent of these coming from China, according to EU Economy Commissioner Valdis Dombrovskis, a trend that is only due to increase.

The number of shipments worth less than €150 coming into the EU have more than tripled in the last two years. Shein and Temu in particular have gained considerable traction in Europe, due to their “pervasive online advertising, low prices and ultra-fast shipping.” The massive influx of parcels makes it difficult for customs to control the millions of packages, as well as make sure they meet the European standard of safety. Not only this, the transport associated with these millions of parcels comes with a hefty environmental impact.

## EU diplomats urging for a change _now_

This decision is part of a larger plan to change EU customs rules, and is expected to take some time to fully implement. The 27 member states will meet again in December of this year to agree on a temporary system that will allow them to implement the change quicker, amid an urgency due to Chinese goods overwhelming the market.

Slovak politician and EU Trade Commissioner Maroš Šefčovič stated that the EU will be ready to implement the change as early as next year, though the plan is currently set to come into force in 2028.

## Chinese e-commerce shops to take a beating

[Shein](https://euroweeklynews.com/2025/07/05/shein-fined-e40-m-for-fake-discounts/) and Temu are set to take a massive hit from the new change, given that their business model relies on selling millions of small packages under the €150 threshold, previously all exempt from customs duty. Ending the exemption would close loopholes and make every package subject to customs duty, disincentivising people from splitting large packages into smaller ones or under-declaring.

## **What does this mean for local expats and business owners?**

For one thing, local businesses and general European manufacturers might benefit greatly from the new overhaul. As Chinese online shops become buyers’ go-to for cheap goods, local shops take a massive hit. A new, tacked-on tariff might be enough to dissuade shoppers from shopping online and encourage them to shop local instead, especially since many items from Shein and Temu are of very questionable quality. In a real store, shoppers will be able to evaluate the goods in person.

With a new incentive to shop locally, expats might find themselves connecting more with local shops, people, and products. Expats, when shopping from Chinese retailers, typically depend on them for kitchenware, clothing, school supplies, and travel accessories. The new tax might encourage shoppers to find local, artisan, or higher-quality products to try that they were previously not aware of or assumed were more expensive. The end of the exemption might also dissuade people from buying cheap, counterfeit versions of legitimate European products.

An increase of local business and a surge in the economy might also bode well for job seekers and improve employment across the board in smaller towns.

As for buyers, though the price tag may not be so sweet anymore after the new change, shoppers might find themselves actually getting _more_ bang for their buck after shopping locally.
