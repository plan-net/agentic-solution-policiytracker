Subject: Proposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND 
OF THE COUNCIL amending Regulation (EU) 2021/1119 establishing the 
framework for achieving climate neutrality
- General approach
I. INTRODUCTION
1. The EU has set its climate targets for 2030 and 2050 in Regulation (EU) 2021/1119 
establishing the framework for achieving climate neutrality (“the European Climate Law”).1
Article 4(3) of the European Climate Law requires an intermediate 2040 climate target to be 
set with a view to achieving the climate-neutrality objective.

2. On 6 February 2024, the Commission published a Communication titled “Securing our future 
- Europe's 2040 climate target and path to climate neutrality by 2050 building a sustainable, 
just and prosperous society”, in which it recommended a 90% net greenhouse gas emission 
reduction compared to 1990 levels as the target for 2040.2
3. On 2 July 2025, the Commission published a proposal3to amend the European Climate Law 
(“the ECL amendment”) by including therein the 90% target referred to above. The 
amendments to the existing Regulation also include three proposed flexibilities to help in 
achieving the EU target as well as a provision whereby the Commission shall aim to 
accelerate and strengthen the enabling framework to ensure that conditions are in place to 
support European industry and citizens throughout the transition.
4. In the European Parliament, Mr Ondřej KNOTEK (PfE, CZ) has been appointed as rapporteur 
for the proposal on behalf of the Committee on the Environment, Climate and Food Safety 
(ENVI). 
5. The Economic and Social Committee adopted its opinion on 18 September 2025.4 The 
Committee of the Regions decided not to issue an opinion.

III. MAIN ELEMENTS OF THE PRESIDENCY COMPROMISE
12. The Presidency compromise text seeks to maintain the main aims and elements of the 
Commission proposal while addressing the positions of delegations expressed on various 
issues. To that end, the text aims to strike a balance between an ambitious climate target for 
2040 and providing sufficient flexibility for Member States in their efforts to achieve that 
target. 
13. The main changes to the initial Commission proposal in successive Presidency compromise 
proposals are summarised below, with references to the relevant articles and recitals. In 
addition, the recitals incorporate several elements based on the guidance provided by the 
European Council in its conclusions of 23 October 2025. The latest Presidency suggestions 
are highlighted in bold. 
a) Flexibilities
‒ Integrating guidance by the European Council on the contribution of international 
credits, namely that the contribution should be adequate as well as both ambitious 
and cost-efficient. Given that the European Council did not provide specific 
guidance on the percentage of the contribution nor its starting year, these aspects 
need to be discussed at the Council and remain in brackets in the present 
compromise text. It is specified in the compromise text that the international 
credits should not play a role for compliance in the EU ETS (Art. 4(5)(a), 
recital 8a). 
‒ Clarifying that domestic permanent removals should compensate for residual 
hard-to-abate emissions rather than for emissions from hard-to-abate sectors (Art. 
4(5)(b), recital 7).

TREE.1.A EN
‒ Specifying that there should be enhanced flexibility both across and within sectors 
and instruments, whereby for example Member States’ achievements in one sector 
can balance gaps in others, and that this should be put in place in a simple and 
cost-effective way (Art. 4(5)(c), recital 8a). 
‒ Integrating guidance by the European Council on the contribution of carbon 
removals to the overall emission reduction effort, namely referring to a realistic 
contribution and ensuring that possible shortfalls would not be at the expense of 
other economic sectors. In the latest Presidency text, it is specified that 
Member States’ surplus natural removals can be used to compensate for 
emissions in other sectors (Art. 4(5)(ca)).
b) Other elements that should be reflected in the proposals for the post-2030 framework:
‒ Strengthening the text on the land use, land use change and forestry sector, 
including by adding references to sustainable and circular bioeconomy as well as 
to considerations on differences in forest age structure, on natural variability and 
uncertainties related to natural disturbances (Art. 4(5)(cb), recital 7).
‒ Reinforcing the text referring to the need to take into account different national 
circumstances and specificities (Art. 4(5)(d)) and the social, economic and 
environmental impacts across Member States, including on aspects of 
competitiveness (Art. 4(5)(f), recital 8a). 
‒ Strengthening the text on the need to ensure a fair and just transition, including by 
reflecting more closely the conclusions of the European Council of October 2025 
and by inserting new elements relating to the need to pay particular attention to 
SME’s, farmers and vulnerable households, ensuring social cohesion, food 
security and a just transition. The latest Presidency text suggests that particular 
attention also needs to be paid the impacts on consumer prices and energy 
and transport poverty (Art. 4(5)(h) and (o), recital 5, 5a).

‒ Including a reference to the reduction of administrative burden (Art. 4(5)(i)), as 
well as highlighting the need to consider climate action from the aspect of 
increased competitiveness (Art. 4(5)(j)) and to strengthen the resilience of the 
Union’s economy and reduce the risk of carbon leakage, including in relations to 
exports (Art. 4(5)(k), recitals 5, 5b).
‒ Strengthening the elements concerning energy, including on availability and
energy security, electricity grids and interconnections with a view to building a 
genuine Energy Union (Art. 4(5)(m), recitals 4, 5a, 5b). 
‒ Specifying in relation to investment needs and opportunities that support for 
innovation and access to innovative technologies should apply across Member 
States (Art. 4(5)(q), recital 5a).
c) Biennial assessment on the implementation of the intermediate targets
‒ The compromise proposal adds to the Commission proposal a requirement for the 
Commission to assess and report biennially on the implementation of the 
intermediate targets laid down in the ECL taking into account the latest scientific 
evidence, technological advances and evolving challenges to and opportunities for 
the EU’s global competitiveness (Art. 4(8)). 
d) Review and revision of the Regulation
‒ The text has been strengthened overall taking account of the guidance by the
European Council, which underlined the need for a revision clause. The text also 
incorporates several requests made by delegations for additional elements to be 
included in the review, such as energy prices, global competitiveness, progress 
towards the intermediate targets and technological advances (Art. 11, first 
paragraph, points (ca) and (d)).

‒ In the latest compromise text, it is specified that the global competitiveness 
challenges and opportunities of European industries need to be considered 
across Member States (point (c)).
‒ The text has also been strengthened to ensure that in light of the uncertainties 
concerning the contribution of removals, in case the Commission finds significant 
divergences between their estimated level and the 2040 target, it shall propose, 
where appropriate, EU-level measures. In the latest Presidency text, it is 
specified that this concerns only natural removals and that the shortfalls 
might be caused by natural disturbances. A provision has also been added 
clarifying that the measures could include an adjustment of the 2040 target 
corresponding to and within the limits of potential shortfalls (point (e)). 
‒ The text on the revision of the Regulation has been strengthened by introducing a 
requirement for the Commission to accompany, where appropriate, the review 
report by legislative proposals, including to revise the intermediate 2040 target
and by additional measures to strengthen the enabling framework (Art. 11, second 
paragraph).
IV. CONCLUSION
14. The Council is invited to agree on a general approach based on the text set out in the Annex to 
this note. The General approach will constitute the Council's mandate for future negotiations 
with the European Parliament in the context of the ordinary legislative procedure.

ANNEX TREE.1.A EN
(2a) In Article 4, the following paragraph is added:
8. Starting from [one year after the adoption of this Regulation] the Commission shall 
biennially assess and report on the implementation of the intermediate targets and 
decarbonisation trajectories set out in this Regulation, taking into account the latest 
scientific evidence, technological advances and evolving challenges to and opportunities 
for the EU’s global competitiveness. The assessment may be accompanied, where 
appropriate, by legislative proposals.
(3) In Article 11, first paragraph, the following points are added: 
‘(c) the evolving challenges to and opportunities for global competitiveness of European 
industries across Member States, in particular of energy-intensive industries and of 
small and medium-sized enterprises, including effects on employment;
(ca) the evolution of energy prices and its impact on European industries and households;
(d) technological advances and deployment across Member States and sectors of innovative 
technologies’;
(e) the estimated level of net removals at Union level in relation to the targets of this 
Regulation. If the Commission finds that the estimated level of net natural removals for 
2040 is significantly diverging from what would be required to achieve the 2040 
intermediate target, including where due to natural disturbances, the Commission 
shall propose, where appropriate, measures at Union level, including if necessary an 
adjustment of the 2040 intermediate target corresponding to and within the limits 
of the possible shortfalls, and ensure that possible shortfalls will not be at the expense 
of other economic sectors;
f) the progress towards the intermediate targets set out in this Regulation.

Article 2
Entry into force
This Regulation shall enter into force on the twentieth day following that of its publication in the 
Official Journal of the European Union.
This Regulation shall be binding in its entirety and directly applicable in all Member States.
