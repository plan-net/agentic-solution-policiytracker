# SHEIN sex-doll scandal sparks EU crackdown

*By Ella Fincken*

Online marketplaces are likely to face new, stringent obligations following the discovery of child-like sex dolls and prohibited weapons for sale on SHEIN’s website. 
 Concerns that the malpractices of online e-commerce platforms such as SHEIN are putting consumers across the EU at risk...

Online marketplaces are likely to face new, stringent obligations following the discovery of child-like sex dolls and prohibited weapons for sale on SHEIN’s website. 
 Concerns that the malpractices of online e-commerce platforms such as SHEIN are putting consumers across the EU at risk have triggered action from both the European Commission and the European Parliament this week. 
 CRACKDOWN 
 In a direct response to a French watchdog’s discovery of child-like sex dolls and other illegal products on SHEIN’s website, the European Commission announced yesterday (26 November) that it has formally requested that the Chinese e-commerce giant provide detailed information and internal documents on how it prevents minors from being exposed to inappropriate content and illegal products from being circulated on its platform. The Commission has also confirmed it has opened a probe into the effectiveness of SHEIN’s mitigation measures. Designated as a very large online platform (VLOP) under the Digital Services Act (DSA) since April 2024, SHEIN has a strict responsibility to assess and adequately mitigate systemic risks arising from its systems or the design and functioning of its services. 
 Acknowledging that the SHEIN scandal in France “stems from the inadequate oversight of e-commerce platforms”, members of the European Parliament (MEPs) adopted a resolution yesterday to address the sale of illegal and unsafe products to EU consumers, particularly from non-EU marketplaces. MEPs have urged both the Commission and EU member states to sideline dialogue and move straight to “timely and decisive enforcement” of the DSA and the General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR). Criticising slow-moving investigations, they also called for online marketplaces’ operations to be temporarily suspended in cases of “repeated, serious or systemic breaches of EU law” with a direct reference to the case in France. 
 “This option should no longer be treated as an exceptional, last-resort measure”, the resolution read. 
 MEPs have further called for dissuasive sanctions, a fast-tracked implementation of the revised Union Customs Code (UCC), and new regulatory reforms and obligations for online marketplaces, citing the need to “close loopholes and ensure these platforms are held accountable for non-compliant goods entering the EU market”. 
 THE SCANDAL 
 On 31 October 2025, the French Directorate General for Competition Policy, Consumer Affairs and Fraud Control (DGCCRF) made the startling discovery that SHEIN was selling sex dolls that resembled children on its website. In reporting its findings to the French public prosecutor and ARCOM – the country’s audiovisual and digital communication regulator – the watchdog noted that the description and categorisation of the dolls left “little doubt as to the child pornography nature of the content” ( Google translation here and hereafter). Pornographic content, including adult sex dolls, was also found on the site without the necessary controls to filter the content from minors, as well as other illegal products such as banned weapons. A formal notice was filed with the platform, calling for the implementation of “appropriate measures” to rectify the situation. 
 The discovery prompted international outrage and fast action from regulators. The Paris prosecutors’ office opened a formal investigation into the platform on 4 November, while the government is currently demanding a three-month suspension of SHEIN’s website in France. 
 THE ONLINE E-COMMERCE BOOM 
 The unprecedented growth of online e-commerce platforms – and the corresponding risks to consumers arising from a steady uptick of illicit practices – has caused a colossal headache for regulators. In July 2025, the European Commission published its preliminary findings that SHEIN’s rival, the Chinese-founded online shopping platform Temu, had violated the DSA by failing to adequately prevent illegal products from being sold on its website. The EU’s executive arm was acutely aware at the time that such practices were not an issue that stopped with Temu. In a July statement on its Temu investigation, it noted: “The steady surge in the volume of products sold online in the EU is accompanied by a rise of unsafe, counterfeit, or non-compliant products.” 
 Regulatory scrutiny of SHEIN extends beyond concerns of the sale of illegal goods. In July 2025, the DGCCRF levied an EUR 40 million penalty against the platform over its deceptive commercial practices and misleading environmental claims, and, in May, the Commission and several national consumer watchdogs banded together to jointly accuse SHEIN of EU consumer protection violations.
