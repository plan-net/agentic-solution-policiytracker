# Shopping phenomenon 'Double 11' is adapting to EU ...

*By Euractiv*

[Ga naar de hoofdinhoud](https://www.euractiv.com/www.euractiv.com#main-content)

[Ga naar de hoofdinhoud](https://www.euractiv.com/www.euractiv.com#main-content)

\[Getty Images: Oscar Wong\]

China’s Singles’ Day has evolved from a quirky celebration of solitude into the world’s most powerful retail event. What began as an informal holiday among university students on 11 November (11/11) – its date symbolising four solitary ones – was transformed in 2009 by Alibaba into a shopping extravaganza.

Today, “Double 11” massively outsizes Western counterparts such as Black Friday and Cyber Monday, generating record-breaking sales and shaping global e-commerce strategies. Once a domestic phenomenon, it now anchors the retail calendar across Asia and increasingly influences markets worldwide.

Dubbed the world’s largest e-commerce event, the Double 11 shopping festival is becoming increasingly visible in Europe, where it intersects with policy debates on competition, consumer protection and digital sovereignty.

What began as a China-centric phenomenon has evolved into a test of how global platforms align with EU standards – and whether international e-commerce flows can strengthen Europe’s competitiveness.

At the centre of this shift sits AliExpress, the Alibaba-owned marketplace serving European consumers directly. Its growing Double 11 footprint offers a real-time illustration of how European shoppers and sellers interact with global platforms shaped by the EU’s regulatory model.

#### **Challenging political assumptions**

Despite political pressure to [“de-risk” from China](https://www.euractiv.com/news/eu-must-de-risk-faster-from-china-says-von-der-leyen/), AliExpress’s usage patterns paint a more nuanced reality.

A Censuswide survey from June 2025 covering six European markets found that more than 80 per cent of users trust the platform and would recommend it. A similar share says their money goes further on AliExpress, and many describe it as a place to find unexpected products.

These trends suggest that European consumers [combine](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/14c842af-f5c3-454a-8d1e-8299bd64697c_en) domestic and cross-border marketplaces, driven by price sensitivity, product diversity and access to international goods – preferences that often advance independently of political discourse.

#### **Double 11 re-engineered**

In recent years, AliExpress has adapted its [European operations](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-makes-aliexpress-commitments-under-digital-services-act-binding) to comply with the [EU’s Digital Services Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/digital-services-act-package) (DSA), which imposes strict obligations on transparency, seller verification and consumer protection.

In 2025, the company received a positive assessment for more than 90 per cent of audited DSA obligations – a sign of how deeply the EU’s rulebook now shapes global platforms.

Operational changes reflect this shift. AliExpress now relies more on EU-based [fulfilment networks](https://www.cep-research.com/2025/06/16/aliexpress-goes-local-in-europe/), enabling 48-hour delivery in many locations, making Double 11 feel increasingly like a European retail moment.

Stronger seller checks and clearer product information are tied directly to DSA requirements and contribute to the high consumer trust recorded in the survey.

European sellers have also become more integrated into the festival. The platform has rolled out regional marketing tools, multilingual interfaces and targeted promotions. Participation has risen steadily, with the number of EU brands on AliExpress tripling in 2024 and their Double 11 sales increasing 2.5 times compared with 2023.

For many SMEs, the event now serves as a practical entry point into cross-border digital commerce.

#### **Europe’s export capacity**

Double 11 is part of a wider commercial system linking European businesses to global consumers. According to [research](https://alizila.oss-us-west-1.aliyuncs.com/uploads/2023/11/SMR-Trade-Report-Alibaba.pdf) by the SDA Bocconi School of Management, exports from 27 European countries to China via Alibaba’s platforms reached €32.3 billion in 2022, totalling €121.4 billion between 2019 and 2022.

In Germany, France, Italy and Spain, these exports supported around 172,600 jobs in 2022 and generated over €6.6 billion in tax revenues.

While AliExpress is not the main export channel in these figures, it operates within the same ecosystem. The platform connects European sellers to more than 150 million global buyers – access that would otherwise be out of reach for many SMEs. Double 11 amplifies this exposure by concentrating demand for international products into a single, highly visible moment.

#### **Openness with oversight**

The EU faces a structural challenge: regulating cross-border digital commerce while ensuring that European consumers and firms can benefit from global markets.

AliExpress’ Double 11 activity exemplifies this balancing act. European users value the platform for convenience and affordability; EU policymakers, meanwhile, demand full compliance with the DSA and other digital standards.

This dynamic unfolds against a complex economic backdrop. A [survey](http://en.ccceu.eu/2025-11/12/c_4799.htm) by Roland Berger for the China Chamber of Commerce to the EU found that Chinese companies operating in Europe believe business conditions have deteriorated for a sixth consecutive year due to political and regulatory uncertainty.

At the same time, Europe’s digital economy continues to expand. Eurostat reports that over three-quarters of Europeans now [buy goods or services online](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20250220-3), and one in four EU enterprises [sells online](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Digital%5Feconomy%5Fand%5Fsociety%5Fstatistics%5F-%5Fenterprises).

For AliExpress, this creates a dual reality: Europe is an attractive, fast-growing digital market, but one governed by some of the world’s strictest rules. Double 11, therefore, becomes more than a retail event – it is a measure of how global platforms adapt to EU expectations, and how Europe balances openness with oversight.

As the EU debates its digital agenda, competitiveness and relationship with China, Double 11 offers a revealing vantage point: a Europe not withdrawing from global e-commerce, but defining the conditions under which it operates.
