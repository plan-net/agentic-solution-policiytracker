# Google Hit With €572M German Court Ruling Over Shopping Monopoly

German court orders Google to pay €572M to price comparison sites for antitrust violations

by [The Tech Buzz](https://www.techbuzz.ai/authors/the-tech-buzz)

PUBLISHED: Fri, Nov 14, 2025, 4:13 PM UTC \| UPDATED: Sat, Nov 15, 2025, 6:31 AM UTC

German court orders [Google](https://google.com) to pay €572M ($665.6M) to two price comparison platforms for antitrust violations

Idealo receives €465M while Producto gets €107M in damages for Google's self-preferencing practices

Ruling builds on 2024 European Court of Justice decision that fined Google $2.7B for similar violations

Google plans to appeal both rulings, claiming its 2017 changes resolved competitive issues

[Google](https://google.com) just took a €572 million ($665.6 million) hit from a German court ruling that found the search giant abused its dominant position in the price comparison market. The landmark decision orders Google to compensate two German shopping platforms - Idealo and Producto - for damages caused by self-preferencing its own Google Shopping service over competitors, marking the latest in a string of major antitrust losses for the tech giant across Europe.

A German court just delivered a crushing €572 million verdict against [Google](https://google.com), finding the search giant systematically crushed competition in the price comparison market. The ruling orders Google to pay €465 million to German shopping platform Idealo and another €107 million to rival service Producto - though both companies are pushing for much more.

The decision sends shockwaves through the tech industry as European courts increasingly target Google's market dominance. Idealo had originally sought €3.3 billion in damages, arguing Google's anti-competitive practices devastated their business for years. The company's legal strategy directly leveraged the [European Court of Justice's 2024 ruling](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/10/google-loses-appeal-against-eus-e2-4b-shopping-antitrust-decision-as-bloc-also-wins-apple-state-aid-appeal/) that found Google violated competition rules by promoting its own shopping service over competitors.

"We welcome the court holding Google accountable. But the consequences of self-favoring go far beyond the amount awarded," Idealo co-founder and CEO Albrecht von Sonntag said in a [company statement](https://www.idealo.de/unternehmen/pressemitteilungen/gericht-bestaetigt-google-hat-idealo-massiv-benachteiligt). "We will continue to fight - because market abuse must have consequences and must not become a lucrative business model that is worthwhile despite fines and compensation payments."

Google isn't backing down. The company immediately announced plans to appeal both rulings, defending its 2017 changes to Google Shopping. "The changes we made in 2017 are working well, with no intervention from the European Commission," a Google spokesperson told [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/google-must-pay-german-price-comparison-platform-465-mln-euros-damages-court-2025-11-14/). The company claims price comparison sites using its Shopping Unit remedy have multiplied from seven to 1,550 across Europe.

But the numbers tell a different story about Google's market grip. The original 2024 European Court ruling hit Google with a $2.7 billion fine for self-preferencing - one of the largest antitrust penalties in tech history. Now German courts are forcing the company to pay actual damages to competitors who claim years of lost revenue.

The timing couldn't be worse for Google's European operations. Just this week, the EU [launched a fresh investigation](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/13/eu-launches-antitrust-probe-into-how-googles-anti-spam-policy-affects-publishers-search-rankings/) into how Google's anti-spam policies affect publisher search rankings. In September, European regulators [slapped Google with another €2.95 billion fine](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/06/eu-fines-google-3-5b-over-adtech-abuse/) for allegedly favoring its own advertising services.

The German ruling exposes a fundamental tension in Google's business model. While the company argues it operates Google Shopping "as if it were a separate business," competitors claim the integration with search results creates an unfair advantage no independent service can match. European regulators increasingly view this as the digital equivalent of a monopolist owning both the marketplace and the stores within it.

For Idealo and Producto, the court victory represents validation after years of legal battles. But with Idealo still pursuing its full €3.3 billion claim, this €572 million ruling might just be the opening salvo in a much larger war over damages from Google's alleged anti-competitive practices.

The broader implications ripple across the entire e-commerce landscape. If German courts are willing to award hundreds of millions in damages for search manipulation, other price comparison services across Europe are likely watching closely. Google's European legal troubles are mounting just as the company faces increased regulatory scrutiny in the U.S. over similar practices.

This €572 million German court ruling marks a turning point in how European courts handle Big Tech antitrust violations - moving beyond regulatory fines to actual competitor compensation. With Google planning appeals and Idealo pushing for its full €3.3 billion claim, this case will test whether European antitrust enforcement can truly level the playing field or if tech giants can simply treat massive penalties as a cost of doing business. For the broader e-commerce industry, it signals that years of alleged anti-competitive practices may finally be catching up with real financial consequences.
