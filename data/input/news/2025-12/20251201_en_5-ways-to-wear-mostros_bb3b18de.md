## 5 ways to wear MostrosMeet the mother of ’00s sneakers

There are Y2K sneakers, and then there’s the PUMA Mostro, a literal monster of a statement shoe with the clout to back it.

29 July 2025 · 3 min read

The PUMA Mostro is an absolute beast of a shoe. Or rather, it’s a monster, by (Italian) name and by nature, famed for ripping up the sneaker rulebooks and offering something completely different. And, in almost three decades, that mindset hasn’t changed at all.

The original released back in 1999 and quickly gained a cult following among the Y2K fashion crowds for being futuristic, totally ahead-of-the-curve, and instantly recognizable. They became a symbol of unconventional, disruptive design, often featured in counterculture mags of the day like _i-D_, _Dazed_, _The Face_, and basically grew to be as synonymous with the era’s zeitgeist as shows like _MTV Cribs_.

Today, the low-profile silhouette has been worn by everyone from Madonna to A$AP Rocky. It’s defined by an asymmetric velcro closure, mesh upper, and spiked rubber soles — a design that fuses ’60s sprint spikes with ’80s surf shoes. And while it may not look it, it’s actually extremely comfortable and versatile, which is just one of the ways the style defies expectations (particularly as comfortable versatility is exactly what we need right now in a summer dominated by structured leather boots and actual football shoes, both of which look great but get pretty painful after a while).

What’s more, the Mostro just dropped on Zalando in eight popping colourways that are guaranteed to liven up anyone suffering from 2025’s summer pastel fatigue. From bright pink to bold yellow to red and all black, there are options here to level up every occasion. And here’s the proof.

Some gym fits are so cute they demand attention off the yoga mat as well as on it. This is one of them. It riffs off the Mostro’s Y2K energy, pulling together pieces that could not be more on point for the era. The elasticated circle mini? The multilayered longsleeve? The pocket waist belt? It’s giving yogalates at 10 and matcha lattes at 11 but with a ’00s indie girl twist.
