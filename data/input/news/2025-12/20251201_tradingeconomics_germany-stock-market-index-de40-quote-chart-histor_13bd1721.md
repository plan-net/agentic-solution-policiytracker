Germany Stock Market Index (DE40)

The DE40 tracks the performance of 40 selected German blue chip stocks traded on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange, which represent around 80 percent of the market capitalization listed in Germany. The Index is free floating and has a base value of 1000 as of December 31, 1987. This index is primarily derived from over-the-counter trading and contracts for difference indexes (CFDs).

**[DAX Edges Higher, Still Poised for Monthly Loss](https://tradingeconomics.com/germany/stock-market/news/505915)**

Germany's DAX closed about 0.3% up at 23,828 on Friday, the highest level since November 14, marking the fifth consecutive session of gains. Market sentiment remained largely supported by hopes of a December Fed rate cut. In Europe, traders eyed inflation data for clues about the ECB's next steps. Preliminary data showed Germany's headline inflation rate came in at 2.3% in November 2025, the same as in October, and below forecasts of 2.4%. Meanwhile, German lawmakers approved the 2026 budget after a prolonged dispute over pension reforms backed by young conservatives. Deutsche Borse led the charge, rising 2.2%, after confirming on Thursday that it is in exclusive talks to acquire the European fund trading platform Allfunds, following earlier reports by Bloomberg. Other top performers included Infineon Technologies, Volkswagen, Deutsche Telekom and Siemens Energy, with gains between 0.8% and 1.7%. The index recorded a 3.2% weekly gain but posted a 0.5% loss for the month.
